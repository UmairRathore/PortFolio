01 / PRODUCT OVERVIEW

Different sports. Different definitions of earnings.

SalaryLeaks tracks athlete salaries, contractual earnings, bonuses, prize money, and career financial information across football, cricket, NBA, NFL, Formula 1, MotoGP, tennis, golf, boxing, and MMA.

The public experience brings together athlete profiles, contracts, rankings, financial comparisons, and team and league information where applicable. Readers can explore financial information through the structure of the relevant sport.

03 / THE MODELING CHALLENGE

A fight purse is not a league contract.

Sports differ in their financial sources, terminology, calculations, and business rules. Football salaries and contractual terms differ from tennis prize money or boxing compensation. Even league-based contracts vary between the NBA, NFL, and cricket.

The central decision: centralize foundational athlete/player and sport entities, while organizing contracts and financial records into sport-specific domains. Share identity and common information without forcing every earnings structure into one generic contract model.
04 / DOMAIN-ORIENTED BACKEND ARCHITECTURE

Shared identity. Deliberate domain separation.

Models, services, and related business logic reflect each sport's financial behavior. This is domain-oriented organization within the Laravel backend. The diagram describes conceptual responsibilities rather than database tables, class names, or deployment topology.

Consistency without uniformity

Shared athlete identities and sport associations remain consistent. Financial behavior stays close to its sport-specific models and services, so a change in one contract domain need not spread into unrelated domains.

Room for new financial structures

New sports and record types can extend the appropriate domain. Ingestion can target the relevant model, and public pages and reporting can accommodate different structures without flattening them into one contract shape.

05 / INGESTION AND NORMALIZATION

Normalize the meaning, not just the format.

I developed and maintained integrations and ingestion workflows pulling data from multiple sources. Historical integrations and tools included:

  • Sportmonks API
  • Capology
  • Transfermarkt
  • Spotrac
  • Playwright
  • Puppeteer
  • Firecrawl

I implemented Playwright-based browser automation for relevant ingestion workflows, alongside external platform integrations and third-party APIs.

  1. Extract for the sourceUse the relevant API integration or source-specific extraction workflow. Browser automation and scraping apply where appropriate to that workflow.
  2. Transform and normalizeInterpret source formats, athlete context, terminology, and financial record types before persistence.
  3. Map into the relevant sportConnect the record to shared athlete and sport information, then persist and process it through the appropriate sport-specific model and services.
  4. Maintain through operationsSupport ongoing data and content maintenance through the application's administration experience.
Historical engineering scope and current editorial practice are distinct. These integrations do not imply that every provider covers every sport or that every record is collected automatically. The current football page describes manual player entry and editorial source checking.
06 / ADMIN AND OPERATIONAL TOOLING

A public data product needs operational care.

The application includes an admin panel for managing sport records, financial data, and content. I developed admin functionality alongside the public website, supporting ongoing data management and production maintenance.

Keeping those tools aligned with the sport-specific models helps operations work with the appropriate financial structures rather than treating all records as interchangeable contracts.

07 / PUBLIC PAGES AND SEO ARCHITECTURE

Make a complex dataset easy to explore.

Sport landing pages lead into athlete profiles, earnings information, contracts, rankings, and comparisons. Team and league pages provide additional context where applicable. SEO-oriented URLs and content hierarchy make sport and athlete information searchable and directly accessible.

Structure follows the sport

Football browsing emphasizes leagues, clubs, and player contracts. Tennis exposes prize-money and earnings information. Motorsports pages present driver and rider agreements, while combat sports focus on purses and compensation.

Interfaces follow the data

Livewire and server-rendered Blade interfaces, with Alpine.js interactions, connect public browsing to the domain model. Comparisons and rankings provide ways to investigate financial information without requiring readers to understand its backend structure.

Reduce repeated work on frequently viewed data.

Database and application

10 / TECHNICAL DECISIONS AND LESSONS

Share the foundation. Preserve the differences.

Put common identity in the shared core

Athlete and sport entities provide consistency across the product. Financial rules belong with the relevant sport rather than being embedded in a universal athlete model.

Keep financial behavior close to its domain

Sport-specific models and services let new record types evolve without spreading conditional contract logic across unrelated sports. The organizing principle is domain-oriented architecture.

Map source data into business meaning

Extraction is only the beginning. Normalization and sport-aware mapping must respect the distinction between a contractual salary, a prize-money record, and fight compensation.

Treat operations and public discovery as part of the system

Administration, searchable page structure, query optimization, caching, and production maintenance all contribute to delivering useful financial information.
